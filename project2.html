Overview

This was a short, collaborative 3D animation project for IAT343 — Animation, created using Autodesk Maya. The concept was a dramatic thriller film in a simple cartoonish style. The plot was inspired by the SCP Foundation, with the atmosphere and general story about foundation agents investigating and raiding a cultist hideout. I was primarily responsible for environment modeling, character rigging, and post-production, such as sound and editing.

Context

Skills and Tools

Autodesk Maya, Mixamo, Microsoft Clipchamp, Storyboarding, 3D Modeling, UV Unwrapping, Texture Painting, Character Rigging, Keyframe Animation, Video & Sound Editing.

Teammates

Selina L., Steven O., Aaron P., Haoyu Z., Shijun G.

Timeframe

3 months

My Role

My role involved modeling and texturing the apartment scene, rigging the protagonist, agents, and cultist, maintaining the project website, putting together the animatic voiceover, and compiling the rendered scenes into a finished animation, including editing and sound effects.

Objective

The main objective of this project was to create a 30 second to 3 minute long animated short, to understand the production process from conception and scripting to storyboarding and animating, through to editing and rendering.

One of the biggest challenges I faced during this project was learning how to cooperate with an unfamiliar group of peers. Learning to work together was necessary for the project to succeed and we accomplished this by communicating our progress and schedule through frequent status updates and meetings. For example, if one member was unable to handle the rendering on their computer, another would volunteer to handle the process, or if someone made a notice that they would be busy with work, it would be fine as long as they were able to meet arranged deadlines for their share of work.

Another challenge to mention from this project was the completely unfamiliar environment we were placed in. I felt that Autodesk Maya was difficult to use effectively as beginners, learning the basics as the course progressed. This led to the end product lacking polish and being unable to reach the high standards I had hoped for.

Process

The very first step of the project was to decide on a concept and what we decided on was a script brought forth by Haoyu. The idea he had in mind was an action thriller where the main character must choose between morals or duty. As an agent of a supernatural investigative strike team tasked with retrieving a target cultist, a dilemma arises when the cultist attacks one of your squadmates. When the operations commander orders you to not interfere but your friend is being strangled in front of you, there is a choice to be made.

With the story decided, we then brainstormed about the style and aesthetics of the animation. We drew inspiration from the cartoonish and simple designs of Team Fortress 2, which can be seen from our concept sketches of characters in figures 2 and 3.

A part of the project requirements was a website to host the project on. I chose and built a Wix website to store our project details. Steven drew the storyboards and I added them to the website, along with details of the scene for filling the storyboard template. The drawings were made on a printed template, scanned, and uploaded online, but pencil handwriting would prove difficult to read, so adding the text digitally in post was more ideal.

With the storyboard as a guide, we began to allocate models, scenes, and shots amongst the team to create. Models we had to create included the different rooms the scenes took place in such as the briefing room- figures 5, 6, and 7, or props and set decoration- figures 8 and 9, or the characters like the commander in figures 10 and 11. One challenge I encountered during this phase was that texture files were not saved as part of the maya file, so we had to manually share the same exact texture file among each other for every model, or else there would be missing textures. We overcame this hurdle by sharing the model as part of a zip file with the textures included.

While making the models, we encountered two more issues, one being the learning curve with Maya's rigging system, and the other being complications with our apartment model.

Figure 12 was an attempt to rig an agent model using Maya's quickrig system but it resulted in the skeleton and skin not binding to the mesh somehow. It took a bit of trial and error but with some help, the workflow we settled into was with using Adobe Mixamo in conjunction with Maya human IK rigging. The base model all of our characters were built from was positioned with their arms down, and I believe that was what was causing issues with the Maya quickrig, as normally models would be in a T-pose position. Adobe Mixamo helped remedy this by repositioning the models into a T-pose and creating a detailed skeleton with fingers shown in figure 13, unlike the Maya quickrig shown in figure 12. Although, the Mixamo skeleton would not be binded to the model, which was resolved using Maya human IK rigging, skinning the model onto the skeleton.

The development of our main location-the cultist's apartment, faced significant complications. The team member initially responsible for the model encountered persistent challenges with UV unwrapping and texture mapping, which caused us to miss internal deadlines. A second member volunteered to address the issues but was also unable to resolve them. At which point I decided to take initiative and fix up the model myself. The before and after can be seen in figures 13 and 14. Beyond adjusting the UV and texture maps, I felt inspired to add environmental storytelling with minor set pieces like a torn up bookshelf, scattered ritual items, and a messy kitchen, to characterize the cultist as deranged and abnormal. My process for these changes is documented in the video below.

After completing the modeling, animating went mostly as according to plan, with scenes divided amongst the team. However, one issue I encountered was that the Maya file had gotten to 200MB, which I believe was the reason Maya would freeze frequently, impeding any progress on my end. I tried to solve this issue on my own, given it was likely my laptop was not up to spec, I tried using school computers to no avail, but I communicated this issue to the team and someone else with a higher-end computer volunteered to help me animate. Once all the scenes were animated and rendered, I compiled them, edited the clips, and added sounds accordingly. Due to prior setbacks, we weren't able to fully realize the idea we conceptualized; lack of time barred us from utilizing motion capture technology and proper voice acting, which we instead replaced with subtitles. The full animated short film can be seen below in figure 17.

Takeaways

From this IAT343 animation project, I learned how to model, texture, rig, and animate in Autodesk Maya, as well as how to cooperate with team members. In the future, I feel like the experience I've gained with Maya will easen the learning curve in future projects. If given the chance to do a similar project, I am confident I would be able to achieve greater results, having already learned the pitfalls and struggles of the software. I also believe I should be ready to take a leadership role, as a lack of roles, left our project without enforced direction, as well as time management- areas that could've been improved.
