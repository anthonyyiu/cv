Overview

This was a project I did for IAT106- Spatial Thinking and Communication called "A Beautiful Machine." I worked with two other peers to create a construct that exercised the ability to think spatially. I was tasked to create a nature-inspired functional machine with two distinct moving parts that was all powered by a crank. One of the main restrictions for this assignment was the limitation of materials to cheap resources such as cardboard and wooden skewers.

Context

Skills and Tools: Spatial design, 3D Modeling with Onshape, and physical prototyping.

Teammates: Daffodil H. and Jako P.

Timeframe: Sep–Dec 2024

My Role: I was responsible for creating the digital model on Onshape as well as the physical construction of the model and linkage mechanisms.

Objective

The main objective of this project was to create a nature-inspired functional machine with two distinct moving parts that was all powered by a crank. It challenged us to think spatially with linkage mechanisms and the restriction of only being able to use cheap materials such as cardboard and wooden skewers.

One of the biggest challenges I faced during this project was ensuring the digital model functioned as intended, because of the complexities of learning Onshape, there was a lot of testing and debugging necessary as something as small as a part misalignment would render the entire model inoperable.

Another problem arose near the end of the project, when our professor challenged us to implement an additional third distinct motion. This was completely unplanned, but I learned to adapt and managed to draft and renovate the model to include an extra bar linkage that attached to the mouth and allowed it to open and close, as can be seen in figure 9.

Process

The concept that was chosen was a whale and the two distinct motions would be the tail wagging and the eruption of a water spout as shown by figure 2. For this project, I created the pieces of the model digitally and assembled them in the 3D modeling website, Onshape, as a proof of concept as can be seen in figure 3.

Figure 4 shows the main bar linkage that serves as the core of the model, with the skewer on the right serving as the crank that powers the entire machine. The longer bar on the left would wag up and down as the shorter right arm rotates, simulating the motion of a whale tail. Figure 5 shows the groundwork of the model housing and the added cams mechanism that would later facilitate the second distinct motion, the spout, in figure 6. Figure 7 shows the finished first version of the whale model with a completed housing container.

It was at this point, after completing the original mission of the project, that we were then tasked by our professor to add an additional function to our model, which we decided would be the opening and closing of the mouth. The housing was already glued shut at this point, but we managed to open it up and attach extra linkage bars to the pre-existing mechanism to get the mouth to move properly.

Takeaways

From this IAT106 "A Beautiful Machine" project, I learned how to draft and test mechanical linkage systems. More importantly, the challenges I encountered have taught me how to adapt to unforeseen difficulties.
